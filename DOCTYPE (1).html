01

Yes and no

Now that you can start pronouncing Sindarin words, it is time for your first vocabulary: greeting friends and strangers, and saying “yes” and “no.”

Much like the Gaelic languages of the United Kingdom and Ireland, Sindarin does not have single words that cover all uses of English “yes” and “no.” Your reply depends on what you are being asked.

AthonYes, I will.
AthofYes, we will.
ûNo; not.
Baw!No, don’t!An emphatic refusal, said with a shake of the head.
The original lesson covers this material in more detail in Week 1 of Introduction to Sindarin. The vocabulary and examples here follow that lesson’s teaching conventions.
02

Ways of saying no

û is the normal way of saying “no” or “not.”

Q

An írog e mass?Do you want the bread?

A

û.No.

Te û edlon.He is not an outsider / stranger.

A stronger refusal

Baw! means the more emphatic “No! Don’t!” You might say it to a naughty child or when particularly upset about something. It is not a general-purpose “no.”

03

Ways of saying yes

Athon and Athof can only answer questions where “Yes, I will” or “Yes, we will” is appropriate. They do not mean “yes” on its own.

Q

Den carathog?Will you do it?

A

Athon.I will.

Q

Den carathodh?Will you all do it?

A

Athof.We will.

Reply by confirming

When “I will” does not fit the question, affirm by repeating the statement or adjusting it to your point of view.

Q

Dagrant in yrch?Did she battle the orcs?

A

Dagrant in yrch.She battled the orcs.

Q

Den serog?Do you like it?

A

Den seron.I like it.

04

Greetings

Siron and Amdiron are friends, so they greet each other informally. Read their conversation, then hide the English to practise.

Between friends

Amdiron

Suilad Siron! Ci mael?Greetings Siron! Are you well?

Siron

Ai, mae govannen! Ni mael, a gin?Hail, well met! I’m well, and you?

Amdiron

Ni mael eithro.I’m well also.

Siron

Ma! Boe menin. Novaer!Good! I must go. Farewell!

Amdiron

Novaer.Farewell.

Vocabulary

SuiladGreetings.
Ci mael?Are you well? / How are you?
AiHail!
Mae govannenWell met.
Ni mael (eithro)I am well (too / also).
A gin?And you?
MaGood!
Boe meninI must go.
NovaerFarewell.Literally, “be well.”
This guide retains Mae govannen as taught in the source lesson. The original page also points readers to an explanation of its approach to the greeting “Mae g’ovannen.”
05

Asking somebody’s name

Amdiron and Ivrinel are meeting for the first time, so they use formal language.

A first meeting

Ivrinel

Mae lovannen! Man en eneth lîn?Well met! What is your name?

Amdiron

Len suilon mellon. En eneth nîn Amdiron. Man en eneth lîn?I greet you, friend. My name is Amdiron. What is your name?

Ivrinel

Im Ivrinel, ach im eston Ivy.I am Ivrinel, but I call myself Ivy.

Amdiron

Gellon len covad. Len nathlon na Imladris!I rejoice to meet you. I welcome you to Imladris (Rivendell)!

Vocabulary

Mae lovannenWell met.
Man en eneth lîn?What is your name?
Len suilonI greet you.
En eneth nîn…My name is…
Im…I am…
achBut.
im eston…I call myself…
Gellon len covadI rejoice to meet you. / I am pleased to meet you.
Len nathlonI welcome you.
na ImladrisTo Rivendell.
06

Formal vs informal

Until you learn the different pronouns, use this comparison as a quick reference. Choose formal language for a stranger or authority figure, and informal language for someone you know well.

FormalA stranger or authority figureInformalSomeone you know wellMeaning
Len suilonGin suilonI greet you.
Mae lovannenMae govannenWell met.
Man en eneth lîn?Man en eneth gîn?What is your name?
A len?A gin?And you?
Le mael?Ci mael?Are you well? / How are you?
“Suilad, mellon.”GREETINGS, FRIEND.
Try it yourself. Introduce yourself using En eneth nîn…, ask your partner’s name, then end your conversation with Novaer!
