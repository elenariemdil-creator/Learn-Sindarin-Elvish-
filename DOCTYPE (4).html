Buying food and drink comes up often in tabletop roleplaying—and in everyday life. To do so, you need to ask for what you would like and respond to someone else.

This lesson is covered in more detail in Week 8 of Introduction to Sindarin. Amdiron, Siron, and Ivrinel are now friends, so they use informal language with one another.
01

Drinking

Beside the fire

Ivrinel

Ai, Amdiron a Siron! Tolo, havo nef e naur a nin.Ah, Amdiron and Siron! Come, sit beside the fire with me.

Amdiron

Gin hannon!Thank you!

Ivrinel

Ci mael?Are you well?

Siron

Ni faug.I’m thirsty.

Ivrinel

Man anírog? Herw, glinen egor nen?What do you want? Wine, mead, or water?

Amdiron

Aníron cherw, gin hannon.I want wine, thank you.

Siron

Nen annin, penin an íred an cherw. Dandolathon lim!Water for me, I don’t want wine. I will return quickly!

Useful phrases

ToloCome.
HavoSit.
Nef e naurBeside the fire.Nef: beside, on this side of. E naur: the fire.
A ninWith me / by me.
Ni faugI’m thirsty.
Man anírog?What do you want?
AníronI want.
Penin íred an…I don’t want…Literally, “I lack desire for…”
AnninFor me.
Dandolathon limI will return quickly.
02

Saying thank you

Even a simple “thank you” has prompted considerable disagreement among Tolkien scholars.

David Salo used Le hannon in the film dialogues. The source lesson adapts this to Len hannon for formal speech and Gin hannon for informal speech.

You may also encounter the newly coined phrase Annon allen. These are proposed expressions, and different learning resources may prefer different forms.

Gin hannonThank you.Informal, as used between friends in this lesson.
Len hannonThank you.Formal, as taught by the source lesson.
I ’ell nînMy pleasure.Literally, “my joy.”
03

Eating

With drinks taken care of, the friends turn their attention to food.

What shall we eat?

Siron

Thî felin haeg. Man mann sevim?Now I’m hungry. What food do we have?

Amdiron

Aes, bass, yf, pae ennas.There is cooked meat, bread, fruit, and vegetables.

Siron

Aníron halph a bass na glî. Ivrinel, man anírog maded?I want soup and bread with honey. Ivrinel, what do you want to eat?

Ivrinel

Cordof annin. Gin hannon Amdiron.An apple for me. Thank you, Amdiron.

Amdiron

I ’ell nîn!My pleasure!

Useful phrases

Felin haegI feel hungry.
Man mann sevim?What food do we have?Mann: food.
Man anírog maded?What do you want to eat?
A bass na glîAnd bread with honey.
Cordof anninAn apple for me.
Reading the source: its vocabulary list also gives Dha for “there is / are.” Some listed dictionary forms differ from the forms appearing in the dialogues; both are retained here rather than silently standardised.
04

Formal vs informal

Before studying verb endings and pronouns in detail, use this comparison to choose the appropriate form of address.

FormalA stranger or authority figureInformalSomeone you know wellEnglish
Man anírol?Man anírog?What do you want?
Len hannonGin hannonThank you.
Den sevil?Den sevig?Do you have / possess it?
Le faug?Ci faug?Are you thirsty?
Le saeg?Ci saeg?Are you hungry?
