“Listen for the change.
One becomes many.”

To write in Sindarin, you need to learn the rules governing plural formation. This lesson covers single vowels and the letter S; the following lesson covers diphthongs such as AE and AI.

The original page notes that this material is covered in more detail in the Intermediate Sindarin course.

Trees, leaves & swords

Ivrinel

In yrn sí vi Imladris û hui yrn vi Lothlorien.The trees here in Rivendell are not like trees in Lothlorien.

Siron

Amman?Why?

Ivrinel

Sevim vellyrn. Ti mellin a gelebrin.We have mallorns. They are golden and silvery.

Siron

Myrn in thys a lais vi Taur-nu-Fuin! Man i naid hain, Amdiron?Bushes and leaves are black in Mirkwood! What are those, Amdiron?

Amdiron

Crist an Edain o Rohan, dagrad yrch a geraif.Swords for Men of Rohan, to battle orcs and wolves.

01

Non-final & final position

The final vowel is the rightmost vowel in a word—not necessarily its last letter. Earlier vowels are in non-final position.

RoseO is non-final; E is final.An English example to illustrate vowel position.
HamA is final: it is the only vowel.

03

Final A → E

Final A normally becomes AI, but changes to E before a consonant cluster of two or more consonants that are not a single consonant sound. Words ending in M and NG also take E; the source gives Cem, “hands.”

Bardh → BerdhHome → homes
Carf → CerfWeapon → weapons
Narn → NernTale → tales
Parch → PerchNaked → plural form
Parf → PerfBook → books
Sarn → SernSmall stone / gem → small stones / gems
Úgarth → ÚgerthMisdeed / sin → misdeeds / sins
04

Final A → I

The source lists six exceptional words whose final A becomes I. Other vowels change normally, except in Fela → Fili.

Fela → FiliSett / boring / tunnel → pluralFili, not Feli.
Madha → MedhiMud → muds
Nadha → NedhiBond / fetter → bonds / fetters
Thala → TheliStalwart / steady / firm → plural form
Thela → Theli*Spearpoint → spearpoints
Tara → TeriTough / stiff → plural form
*The supplied page text omits the plural after Thela. Theli is supplied here by applying the rule stated in the lesson.
05

Non-final O in compounds

In a compound—a word made from two or more words—the source teaches that the first O does not change.

Morchant → MorchaintShadow cast by light → shadows cast by light
Rochben → RochbinHorselord / rider of Rohan → plural
06

Final O → UI / Y

The source presents evidence suggesting that monosyllabic words with final O should take UI rather than Y. Words with more than one syllable retain the final O → Y pattern.

Thôn → ThuinPine tree → pine treesOne syllable.
Roch → RuichHorse → horsesOne syllable.
Thoron → TherynEagle → eaglesMore than one syllable; the non-final O also changes.
07

Final SS → S

SS becomes S only at the end of a word. Internal SS remains unchanged, as in Lossen, “snowy.”

Because final SS reduces to a single consonant, it counts as one consonant when deciding whether final A becomes E or AI.

Rass → RaisHorn → hornsRais, not Res.
Lass → LaisLeaf → leaves
