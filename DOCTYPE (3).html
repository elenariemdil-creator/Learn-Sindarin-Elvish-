Basic interrogatives—the words for “who,” “what,” “why,” and so on—are useful building blocks for sentences. The verb endings and prepositions introduced here will be covered in detail in later lessons.

This lesson is covered in more detail in Week 7 of Introduction to Sindarin.

An important distinction: the source identifies Man as the only attested Sindarin interrogative. The other question words presented here are reconstructed.

Practise reading the examples without their translations.

01

What? Who?

The word Man means both “what” and “who.” Context helps distinguish the two meanings.

Man te?What is it? / Who is it?
Man sa?What is that? / Who is that?
Man e lû?What is the time?
Man sad?What place?An alternative for “Where?”
Man agoreg?What did you do?
Man na e fen?Who is at the door?
To avoid ambiguity when asking “Who is it?”, the lesson offers Man pen?—“What person?”
02

Where?

Choose the question according to whether you mean “in,” “at,” or “from” a place.

Mivan?In what place?
Navan?At what place?
Oman?From what place?
Mivan i degil nîn?Where is my pen?In what place is my pen?
Navan agoreg?Where did you do that?At what place did you do that?
Navan e mar?Where is the house?
Navan ci?Where are you?When you are not within the same locale.
Mivan ci?Where are you?When you are within the same locale or building.
Oman i ost tôl?Where is the city he is coming from?The source glosses this as “From what place is the city he is coming?”

You have already met Navan in Lesson 3: Navan dorthog?—“Where do you live?”

Alternative: Mas? is another proposed word for “Where?”, reconstructed from Quenya.
03

When?

Mallú?When?
Mallú agoreg?When did you do that?
Mallú ci onnen?When were you born?
Mallú iavas?When is autumn?
Mallú telithar?When will they come?
Alternatives: Na van lû? means “At what time?” The source also lists Mar?, a proposed “When?” reconstructed from Quenya.
04

How?

Manen?How?
Manen agoreg?How did you do that?
Manen istog?How do you know?
Manen ven hirnig?How did you find us?
Alternative: Naman?—“How?”, literally “With what?”
05

Why?

Amman?Why?
Amman níniol?Why are you crying?
Amman agoreg?Why did you do that?
Amman en ell luin?Why is the sky blue?
Amman gelia Edhellen?Why is he studying Elvish?
06

For what purpose?

To ask specifically about purpose, the lesson uses Amman theled rather than the more general “Why?”

Amman theled agoreg?Why did you do that?For what purpose did you do that?
Amman theled anírog istol?Why do you want to know?For what purpose do you want to know?
07

Other questions

Combine question words with prepositions to ask more specific questions.

O van?From what? / About what?
Be van?According to whom?

The original lesson points ahead to Lesson 13 for more prepositions you can use to form questions.
