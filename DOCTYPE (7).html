“A small word.
A sense of who we are.”

This lesson introduces indefinite pronouns such as “someone,” nominative forms used in statements such as “I am,” and emphatic or reflexive forms that can express “I myself” or “yourself.”

01

Indefinite pronouns

Use these words when the person, thing, or place is unspecified.

NadSomething
Únad / AlnadNothing
PenSomeone / somebody
Úben / AlphenNo one / nobody
SadSomewhere
Úhad / AlhadNowhere
02

Nominative pronouns

In the source lesson’s examples, a nominative pronoun followed by a description forms a statement such as “I am old.” No separate word for “am,” “is,” or “are” appears.

Singular meaningFormPlural meaningForm
I amniWe areme
You are — formalleYou all are — formalde
You are — informalciYou all are — informalci
He / she / it is; singular they areteThey all areti
This isseThese aresi
That issaThose aresai
Important: the source states that the word immediately following a nominative or emphatic pronoun does not mutate.

Examples

Ni brûnI am old.
Le laichYou are sweet.
Te ithronHe is a wizard.
Sai yrnThose are trees.
03

Emphatic & reflexive

These forms can add emphasis to the person being identified or express a reflexive meaning such as “yourself.”

ImI myself; myself / ourselvesThe source uses the same form for singular and plural.
EchYou yourself; yourself / yourselves
E / EstHe / she / it; himself / herself / itselfThe source states that singular and plural share the same forms.
E or Est? The lesson presents both as options, not as forms chosen according to whether the following word begins with a vowel.

More than a simple introduction

Nominative

Ni BoromirI am Boromir.

Emphatic

Im BoromirI, myself, am Boromir.

The emphatic form adds an extra sense of personal importance. The source illustrates this with the inscription on the doors of Moria:

Im Narvi, hain echant“I, Narvi, made them.” — The Lord of the Rings

The emphasis is not simply “I made them,” but “I, myself, made them.” You can also use an emphatic form to introduce someone else:

E / Est Gandalf, i ithron vithHe is Gandalf, the grey wizard.
04

Asking questions

To turn these statements into questions, add a question mark when writing and speak the sentence as a question. The pronoun remains the same.

Statement

Ni brûnI am old.

Question

Ni brûn?Am I old?

Te ithron?Is he a wizard?
Sai yrn?Are those trees?
Keep the sentence intact. In these examples, you do not reverse the order of the words as English does with “I am” → “Am I?”
